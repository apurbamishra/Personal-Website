About

Two halves of the same interest

I'm a Computing student at Leeds Beckett University, working toward a BSc (Hons) and expecting to graduate in 2027. Most of what I know, I've learned by building: console tools in C++, small utilities in Python, interfaces in JavaScript, and a responsive multi-page site from scratch. Each one was an excuse to understand a language or an idea properly rather than just finish a module.

Alongside the code, I write. During my internship I wrote blog posts, website copy and short-form video scripts, and I learned that the most useful thing I do isn't tied to one language — it's taking something technical and explaining it so that someone outside the field actually gets it.

That combination is what I'm building on. I'm interested in web development and UI/UX, I'm working my way into AI and machine learning, and I do my best work on projects where I get to think about both how something works and how it's explained.

Turning technical things into plain language

A lot of my work sits between the people who build something and the people who need to understand it. In my internship that meant blog posts, website copy, social content and short-form video scripts across tech, business and education — written for a student and EdTech audience, and kept inside one brand voice across two companies.

It's the same instinct that makes me a better developer: if I can't explain what a piece of code does in a sentence, I probably don't understand it well enough yet.

How a short script comes together

  1. 1

    Hook

    Earn the first three seconds, or nothing after them matters.

  2. 2

    Context

    Say who this is for and why the topic is worth their minute.

  3. 3

    Explain simply

    Strip the jargon, keep the accuracy. Analogy before terminology.

  4. 4

    Takeaway

    Leave one idea the viewer can repeat to someone else.
